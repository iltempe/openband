# Changelog

All notable changes to OpenBand. The v0.2 work follows [docs/SPEC.md](docs/SPEC.md).

## v0.6.21 "G2a: draw the song — the arranger"

- v0.6.20 has no release: its release check failed on a test that compared two packed versions byte for byte when the two saves fell in different seconds (gzip writes a timestamp in its header). Versions now pack without one, so the same song always gives the same bytes. Everything below was in v0.6.20 and ships here.

### G2 (part 1) · From an energy curve to a whole song
- **New MCP tools (146 in all)**: **`set_energy_curve`** (points `{bar, energy 0..1}`, or a shape — `pop`, `slow_build`, `edm_drop`, `ballad` — for `length_bars` or `seconds`, e.g. 3:30 at the song's tempo), **`get_energy`** (the drawn curve and the song's measured one, per bar), **`arrange_from_energy`** (`seed`, `genre`, `as_branch` = true, `keep_sections`, `modulate`) and **`add_transition`** (`riser`, `downlifter`, `reverse_cymbal`, `fill`, `stop`, `impact` at a bar). The curve is saved in the song (`Project.energy`).
- **The arranger** (`src/arranger/`, pure, deterministic for a seed): the curve is cut into 4/8/16-bar sections where it changes and labelled (intro at a quiet start, choruses on the peaks, a bridge in the valley after the second chorus, a pre-chorus on a short rise, outro at the end; always a verse and a chorus). Each section gets the layers its energy calls for, by role (harmony first, then texture and vocals, lead, bass, drums); the second verse gets one more layer, or a busier groove when everything already plays. The drummer's part is played again per section, busier and louder with the energy and with fills before rises; the lead is doubled an octave up on the peaks (a track of its own); the tone opens through build-ups; the reverb rises in the valleys; transitions where the energy jumps (riser, reverse cymbal, impact, downlifter, a fill, a bar of **stop** before a big drop); and an ending — a fade when the curve falls, the tonic chord with a crash when it doesn't. `modulate` raises the last chorus (copies only).
- **Transitions are synthesized** on the spot (seeded noise through a sweeping band-pass with a rising tone, its reverse, a high-passed burst played backwards, a falling sine boom with a hit) and stored as samples on an "FX transitions" track.
- **The user's notes are never changed**: their regions are moved to where their track first plays and copied (or shortened) elsewhere; only generated parts (drummer, octave doubling, fills, last chord, transitions) are new. The result goes on a branch `arrangement-<n>`, opened in compare view against the song as it was.
- Fixed: v0.6.19 has no macOS installers. On the CI macOS VM the packaged-app smoke heard the CLAP gain at −7.69 dB (tolerance 1.5): every level heard live is now among the live checks reported but not required there; latencies, crash handling, exports and the render cache stay required. The end-to-end tests run Chrome without background timer throttling (an offline render could crawl for minutes), and the monitoring test holds its notes long enough to reach a period on a busy machine.
- Not yet: the drawing lane in the studio and the *Arrange* button (part 2); block → stab → arpeggio for chord parts (chord parts are copied as written).
- Tests (the spec's acceptance): from an 8-bar loop with drums, bass, chords and lead, all four shapes give a 64-bar song with verse, chorus and outro that follows the drawn curve — **correlation 0.87 (pop), 0.88 (slow build), 0.92 (EDM), 0.93 (ballad)** (bound 0.7); the same seed gives the same song; the user's regions keep their notes (also with hand-written drums); the second verse adds the drums; transitions land on their own audio track; the synthesized transitions rise, fall and hit as they should and are always the same. In Chrome (`npm run e2e`, *arrange*): the arrangement opens on `arrangement-1` in compare view, intro · verse · chorus · verse · chorus · bridge · chorus · outro over 48 bars, correlation 0.80, the user's notes unchanged, 10 transitions rendered to samples.

## v0.6.19 "G3b: ghost notes in the piano roll"

### G3 (part 2) · Tab to write
- **Ghost notes in the editor**: 600 ms after the last edit, the piano roll shows OpenBand's suggestion in grey with **1/3** and the keys — **Tab** accepts it as **one undo step**, **⇧Tab** shows the next, **Esc** dismisses it until the region changes. The kind fits the region: continue a melody, a bass line on an empty bass region under chords, a variation of the track's motif on an empty section; in the **step sequencer** the rest of the groove (or a fill), as grey cells. *Continue*, *Harmonize* (the chords for a new harmony track, shown in violet, Tab creates the track), *Counter-melody* and *Bass line* ask for one now; **?** shows the reasons, in English or Italian. A **Suggestions** switch in the editor (on by default).
- **It learns from you**: what you accept and dismiss is counted locally; ideas that repeat your motif rise or fall with it. *Forget what I taught* starts again.
- **Jam mode answers at once**: when you play a phrase, the same engine continues it on the next window while the AI thinks; if the AI's answer arrives before OpenBand's has started, it replaces it.
- Website: a card for ghost notes.
- Not done: training on the user's other songs (the app keeps one song at a time; other songs live in files).
- Tests: in Chrome (`npm run e2e`, *ghosts*) the suggestion appears after the pause, *?* gives its reasons, ⇧Tab shows 2/3, Esc dismisses it and it doesn't come back until an edit, Tab adds its notes (7 → 10) and **one undo** takes them back (7), *Harmonize* right after an edit shows its chords (the automatic suggestion doesn't replace it) and Tab puts them on a new Harmony track. Unit: every reason has its Italian, the jam answer starts on its bar line in the next chord.

## v0.6.18 "G3a: ghost notes, the engine"

### G3 (part 1) · A musical autocomplete, local and instant
- **`suggest_continuation`** (MCP, **142** tools): for a region, OpenBand suggests what comes next — continue a melody (the rest of the bar and the next), a **bass line** under the chords (roots, root–fifth, walking with passing notes, octaves), **harmonize** a melody (3 chord progressions, voiced for a new harmony track), a **counter-melody** (contrary motion, never doubling the melody, no parallel fifths or octaves), a **variation** of the track's motif for an empty section (a third higher, a step lower, inverted, a fourth higher — same rhythm), and for drums the **rest of the groove** or a **fill** with a crash after it. The kind is picked from the context when not given; every suggestion says why ("chord tones of Am on the strong beats", "repeats the rhythm of bar 2").
- **No LLM, no network, no key**: harmonic rules (chord tones on beats 1 and 3, scale tones in between, the chords read from the other tracks) and light variable-order Markov models — (interval, duration) for melodies, scale degrees for chords — trained on the song itself first (×5), then on a small corpus written for OpenBand and released as CC0 (40 melodies, 30 progressions; key-free, in scale steps). Ranking: model probability + harmony + singable range (leaps over a fifth and spans over an octave and a half cost) + variety; the motif of the last bar is repeated with variation in half the candidates.
- Spec deviations, for now: it runs in the page, not in a worker (3 ms at p95 — a worker would add its round trip); the user's other songs are not part of the training yet; the piano roll UI (ghost notes, Tab / ⇧Tab / Esc, learning from your choices) is part 2.
- Tests (the spec's acceptance): **p95 3.0 ms** for 3 suggestions on a 64-note region (bound 50 ms); over 30 seeds on a harmonic fixture **126 of 126** strong-beat notes are chord tones (bound 90 %); **no fetch** while suggesting, for every kind; the same seed gives the same ideas, three different ones; the bass follows A F C G on the downbeats; harmonizations keep each bar's downbeat note in its chord; the variation keeps the rhythm; the drums continue the groove and fill.

## v0.6.15 "G8.8: the bridge, measured"

### G8.8 · Shared memory for the bridge — measured, not adopted
- The native plugin bridge now measures itself: the jitter buffer counts how many blocks each block takes to come back (the round trip, in blocks of 128 frames), and `bridgeStats()` gives p50, p99 and max. The packaged-app smoke test reports it on every platform.
- **Measured** (packaged app, a CLAP effect): this Mac p50 0 / p99 1 / max 1 block; CI macOS 1 / 1 / 2; CI Windows 1 / 3 / 3; CI Linux 1 / 3 / 3 (one block is 2.7 ms at 48 kHz). The pipes between the main process and the plugin host, alone: p50 0.06 ms, p99 0.36 ms (3000 blocks at real-time pace).
- **Not adopted**, as the spec allows ("kept only if it improves p99"): shared memory between the main process and the host can only remove the pipe leg — a tenth of a block at p99 — so it cannot improve a p99 measured in blocks. Shared memory all the way to the AudioWorklet needs a SharedArrayBuffer over memory mapped from the host (a V8 addon per Electron version and platform) and a cross-origin-isolated page, which would stop web plugins loading from their CDNs. The numbers show where the latency is instead: the 6-block jitter buffer is twice what the slowest platform needs. Details in [ADR 0001](docs/adr/0001-desktop.md).
- Tests: the lateness histogram and its percentiles (unit); the smoke reports `bridge` on macOS, Windows and Linux.

## v0.6.14 "G8.7: low-latency monitoring"

### G8.7 · Monitoring a native instrument with only the device's latency
- **Low-latency monitoring** (desktop app): when the track you play (the selected one) has a native instrument, `openband-plughost` opens the computer's audio output itself — CoreAudio, WASAPI, ALSA / PulseAudio, through [miniaudio](https://github.com/mackron/miniaudio) (public domain / MIT-0) — with a 128-frame period, and runs the plugin in its callback. The keyboard's notes go straight to it (a message from the page to the host, no audio bridge), so the player hears the device's latency instead of the bridge's ~16 ms. The track's timeline notes play there too, scheduled later by what a block takes through the bridge and the studio's output, so they stay in time with the rest of the song; the blocks the studio receives come back silent meanwhile. The track's fader (mute included) and pan apply, as the studio's channel does them (mono, equal-power); its other effects and the master chain don't while it's on.
- Smart Controls of a native instrument: a **Low-latency monitoring** switch (on by default, remembered), *Low-latency monitoring on* with the device and its buffer, and the last measured key-to-sound time. It follows the selected track, its fader and pan, and a plugin restarted after a crash.
- The host measures every live note: frames from its arrival to its first sample (`wait` + `offset`), plus the device buffer (`total`), sent as `HEARD`.
- Spec deviation: CPAL is a Rust library, and the host is C/C++ (the Rust toolchain is not used here), so the device layer is miniaudio. The MIDI keyboard still reaches the host through the page (Web MIDI → IPC → host, well under a millisecond) rather than by the host opening MIDI itself; the rest of the mix is not routed into the host's output (the system mixes the two outputs).
- Tests: on miniaudio's null device (every platform, CI included) live notes are heard on the first sample of their period, the blocks come back silent while monitoring and with sound after; in the packaged app (`desktop:smoke`) monitoring starts for the selected native instrument on the null device, a key is heard, and it stops. On this Mac's real output (`OPENBAND_TEST_REAL_AUDIO=1`, muted), key → sound measured **133–173 frames with a 64-frame period (bound: buffer 128 + 2 blocks = 384)** and **257–380 frames with 128 (bound 512)** — the spec's acceptance.

## v0.6.13 "G8.6: Audio Units"

### G8.6 · AU (macOS)
- **Audio Unit instruments and effects** work in the desktop app on a Mac, like CLAP and VST3 and behind the same bridge: the plugin browser lists the AUs the system knows (as Logic or GarageBand see them, Apple's own included) with an **AU** badge — read from the component registry, without loading any of them, so a broken AU can't hang the scan. Each runs in its own process, with its latency compensated (`kAudioUnitProperty_Latency`), MIDI notes on their sample, parameters in the AU's own ranges and units, automation, the AU's own view (or the system's generic one) as the plugin window, its controls recorded in Touch / Latch and undo (`AUEventListener`), its state saved with the song (ClassInfo, a binary property list), offline exports (`kAudioUnitProperty_OfflineRender`) and the render cache.
- **Sample-accurate even when the AU isn't**: many AUs apply a parameter for a whole render call (Apple's AUSampleDelay does), so the host renders each block in pieces, cut where a parameter changes.
- How: `native/plughost/src/au.mm` wraps an AU as a CLAP plugin inside `openband-plughost`, as `vst3.cpp` does for VST3; AUs have the path `au:` and the id `type:subtype:manufacturer` (e.g. `aufx:dely:appl`).
- Tests (on every Mac, CI included, with Apple's AUs): the registry lists AUDelay and DLSMusicDevice without loading them; AUPeakLimiter's impulse comes out exactly as late as its reported latency (96 samples); AUSampleDelay's delay changed at sample 64 leaves an impulse at 20 alone and moves the one at 70 to 80; state round trip; DLSMusicDevice plays a note from its sample on, and twice the same offline; an unknown id. In the packaged app (`desktop:smoke`, macOS): AUPeakLimiter on a track, level unchanged (0.05 dB), latency compensated (768 + 96).
- Not yet: AUv3 plugins that run only as app extensions are untested.

## v0.6.9 "G8.4: native plugins in exports, and songs that travel"

### G8.4 · Offline export of native plugins, render cache
- **Exports with native plugins** (desktop app): an export runs each native plugin in a host of its own, in the plugin's **offline mode** (CLAP `render`), and the render pauses every 8 blocks to send them through it and wait for them back (the worklet acknowledges every batch), so no block is ever late: **two exports are identical to the bit**. Plugin latency (8 blocks + the plugin's own) is compensated as live; instruments count too. Native automation is applied in exports as live, on its sample. Until now an export played native tracks without them.
- **Render cache** (`Track.frozen`, `src/audio/freeze.ts`, `src/freezeTracks.ts`): saving a song as `.openband` in the desktop app freezes each track that plays a native plugin — rendered alone, after its effects and before its channel, at 48 kHz — and keeps it with the song's samples, only for the tracks whose notes, sound (plugin state), effects or automation changed (a fingerprint). Where the plugins can't run (the browser, the online studio, a computer without them), the track plays its frozen audio through its channel — fader, pan, sends and mute stay live — with **❄ Frozen** next to its name; after its notes change, **Out of date**, and the badge opens the plugin browser to pick another instrument. Exports use it too.
- New render option `tap`: a track after its effects and before its channel (the render cache) — playing it back through the channel sounds like the track itself, not 3 dB under (Tone's channel sums to mono with the equal-power pan law).
- The spec's FLAC/Opus compression of the cache is not there yet: it is stored as a 16-bit WAV.
- Tests: the fingerprint (notes and sound in, the mix out), when the frozen audio plays (browser; desktop only if the plugin failed), the freeze view. In the packaged app (`desktop:smoke`): two exports of a song with a native instrument and its automation are **bit-identical**, the export has the instrument (−12 dB) and matches live playback (per-100 ms windows, max 0.00 dB), freezing renders both native tracks with the right fingerprint. In Chrome (`npm run e2e`, *frozen*): a song with a CLAP instrument plays its render cache live and in the export (same level, through the track's channel), shows ❄ Frozen, and *Out of date* after its notes change.

## v0.6.5 "G8.2: CLAP effects in the desktop app"

### G8.2 · openband-plughost, plugin scan, CLAP effects, crash isolation
- **`openband-plughost`** (`native/plughost`, C11 + the CLAP 1.2.6 headers, MIT): the native plugin host, one process per plugin instance (the spec's safe mode). A framed binary protocol over stdin/stdout — load, 128-frame blocks, parameters, state save/load, latency changes — and a `scan` mode that lists the plugins of a bundle. Built with CMake by `scripts/build-plughost.mjs` (macOS: one universal binary; Windows and Linux: x64 and arm64) and shipped inside the desktop installers. Written in C rather than Rust (the ADR allows either): it builds on every CI runner and the VST3 SDK it will meet next is C++.
- **Scan** (`server/plughost.mjs`): the standard CLAP folders of each system plus folders the user adds; every bundle in a process of its own with a timeout, so a plugin that hangs or crashes lands on the ignore list with the reason and a *Retry*; cache by path and modification time.
- **CLAP effects on a track** (desktop app): the plugin browser lists the installed plugins (*Installed on this computer*, with a format badge and search) next to the WAM catalog. The main process runs each plugin's host and hands its audio a MessagePort straight to the plugin's AudioWorklet, so blocks never pass through the page's main thread; latency = 6 blocks + the plugin's own, compensated (G8.1). Plugins load only from the plugin folders, never from a URL, and a warning comes before the first one.
- **Crash isolation**: when a plugin crashes — or stops answering for more than 2× the jitter buffer — the worklet plays the track bypassed **with the same latency**, so it stays in time and the audio never stops; the slot says *Closed* and **Restart** starts the plugin again with the state saved in the song.
- Fixed: replacing a plugin in the same effect slot left the old one wired in (the chain only rewired when the slot ids changed).
- The plugin browser is just *Plugins* now (WAM and native).

## v0.6.4 "G8.1: plugin delay compensation"

The native plugins of the desktop app (G8) come in phases; this one makes room for them in the engine.

### G8.1 · Plugin delay compensation, NativePluginNode
- **Plugin delay compensation** (live in `engine.ts`, offline in `render.ts`): every effect chain reports its latency — WAM plugins through `getCompensationDelay()` (WAM 2), native plugins through their node — and every other track is delayed by the difference, so the slowest track sets the pace and everything stays aligned. The offline render runs long enough for up to 1 s of plugin latency and trims exactly what was added. The effect slot shows the latency it compensates (*26.7 ms*). Sidechain keys are taken before compensation.
- **NativePluginNode** (`src/audio/native.ts`, `worklets/native.worklet.ts`): a native plugin in the Web Audio graph, as an AudioWorklet that sends each 128-frame block to the plugin host and plays what comes back through a jitter buffer of 6 blocks (~16 ms, the ADR's measurements; late blocks play as silence and are counted), so its latency is exact: 6 × 128 + the plugin's own. Until `openband-plughost` runs (G8.2), the host is **in-process**: two test plugins (`native-dsp.js`: a pure latency and a gain), processed on the page behind the same bridge, and inline in the worklet for offline renders.
- **Plugin formats in the project**: `PluginSlot` and `CustomInstrument` get `format` (`wam`, `clap`, `vst3`, `au`, `test`) and `native` (id, vendor, version, path hint, state). Projects saved before are migrated to `format: 'wam'` (same schema version, so older OpenBands still open new songs). The effect slot shows a format badge; the DAWproject report names the format.
- The effects panel wraps onto a second row instead of scrolling the plugins out of sight.
- Tests: the test plugins (latency exact block by block, gain), the jitter buffer (N blocks later, late blocks counted), the format migration. In Chrome (`npm run e2e`, *pdc*): two identical notes, one through 512 samples of plugin latency, line up **0 samples** apart in the export and live (where the compensation is 1280 samples: 6 bridge blocks + 512), and 1280 apart with compensation off.

## v0.6.3 "G1b: the Mix & Master Pilot"

### G1 (part 2) · Mix & Master Pilot, export for platforms
- **Mix Pilot** (new *Mix Pilot* tab; `mix_pilot`): a genre (pop, rock, hip-hop, lo-fi, EDM, acoustic, ambient, jazz — `src/pilot/profiles/*.json`), a platform and **one knob**. ## v0.6.2 "G1a: a master chain that hits the target"

### G1 (part 1) · Master chain, true-peak limiter, sidechain, high-pass
- **Master chain** (Mixer, `set_master_chain`): EQ (tilt in dB/octave around 1 kHz, low and high shelves, peaking bands) → **glue** compressor → **width** (mid/side, with everything below *Mono below* kept in mono) → **true-peak limiter**. Stages that are off leave the signal path. One class (`src/audio/master.ts`) runs live and in the offline render, so what you hear is what you export.
- **True-peak limiter** (`src/audio/worklets/master-dsp.js`, an AudioWorklet): 4× oversampled peak detection, 5 ms lookahead, a gain ramp that reaches full reduction exactly when the peak plays (sliding minimum + moving average, so the ceiling holds), release that slows down when the reduction lasts. Its latency (5 ms + 12 samples) is compensated exactly in exports. Gain-reduction meter in the panel.
- **Set loudness / platform targets**: Spotify −14, Apple −16, YouTube −14, SoundCloud −11 LUFS at −1 dBTP, Club −8, CD −9 at −0.5. OpenBand renders the song and finds the limiter gain that lands within ±0.3 LU (secant method: under heavy limiting a dB in is less than a dB out; at most 4 renders), and says when a target is out of reach for the material.
- **Sidechain** (`set_track_effects` → `compressor.sidechain`, and *Sidechain from* in the compressor): a track's compressor keyed by another track, pre-fader and pre-mute — the bass ducks under the kick even with the kick muted, live and in exports (sources that aren't audible are still rendered as keys).
- **High-pass** per track (12 dB/oct, before the EQ; `eq.hpf`).
- **A deterministic compressor**: track compressors and the glue now run OpenBand's own compressor (soft knee, peak detector, no hidden make-up gain) instead of the browser's DynamicsCompressor, whose automatic make-up and block-dependent behaviour made live and export differ. **Songs that use the compressor export at a different level than before** (usually a few dB quieter: what *Makeup* says is now what you get).
- **Fixed: mute during playback.** Muting a track live had stopped working (a volume change undid it); mute now acts on the track's own gain stage. Exports were not affected.
- The live master capture (`captureMaster`, used by tests and level matching) runs on an AudioWorklet instead of a ScriptProcessor that dropped blocks under load.
- Tests: the limiter on three fixtures (EDM, pad, pluck) with per-genre targets — ceiling never passed on the 4× true peak, LUFS within tolerance, transparent below threshold, exact latency, no clicks; compressor static curve and make-up; ducking; independence from the block size. In Chrome (`npm run e2e`, *master*): `set_master_chain` for Spotify measured independently on the export (−14.2 LUFS, −1.0 dBTP), live mute silent, live = export within 1 dB, the kick ducking the bass by 16 dB in a stem without the kick; and through the UI, *Set loudness* → −14.0 LUFS, −1.0 dBTP.

## v0.6.1 "Ensemble E1: OpenBand plays with engines"

### E1 · Engine host, router, conformance, ledger, builtin engines
- **✦ Create** (toolbar and the new *Ensemble* tab; `create` for AI clients): describe a part — *“funk drums”*, *“arpeggiated piano”*, *“a lo-fi keys loop”* — and where (song, cycle, section, bars); OpenBand infers the capability (drums, accompaniment, audio layer), routes it to an engine, sends the **Song Context Package** (`src/ensemble/context.ts`: tempo, key, sections, the harmony read from the notes, track roles, lyrics, a SHA-256 of the canonical song — trimmed to what each engine declares it uses), conforms the result and inserts it as **one undo step**; variants (up to 4, rotated across engines, with fallback on failure) become takes of one region, ready for a blind test. - **Conformance, steps 1–4 and 9** (`src/ensemble/conform.ts`): claims checked against measurements; notes moved into the song's key when that puts them back in key, into the range by octaves, cut to the scope; audio stretched to the tempo (F23, non-destructive), shifted to the nearest compatible key, repeated or trimmed to fill the bars. Every correction is recorded in plain words. The tempo of audio is taken from its **duration** when it confirms the engine's claim (onset detection misreads swung grooves by ratios like 1.4), and a declared key wins over audio key analysis (fragile on bass lines); both checked on all 16 library loops.

- Input device selection (`enumerateDevices`) in the microphone menu; the recording worklet now forwards every channel of the device (`getUserMedia({channelCount})`, discrete channels), and each armed track takes its own: e.g. input 1 → Vocal, input 2 → Guitar.
- **Arm** per audio track (the **R** button in the track header): several armed tracks record together in one pass, each into its own clip.
- **Punch in/out** on a bar range with a **pre-roll** of N bars (the song plays, the recording starts at the punch-in and stops at the punch-out; the song's loop is suspended meanwhile). Over an existing clip the result is a new take of that clip — the old audio with only the punched range replaced, joined by 5 ms equal-power crossfades (`src/punch.ts`, pure) — else a new clip. A red band shows the range on the ruler.
- **Loop recording**: with the cycle on (and no punch), every pass becomes a take of one clip over the cycle, the last one active (F12).
- Optional **input monitoring**, with a warning when the device's latency is noticeable (over 12 ms).
- Arming, punch and device are session settings (not undo steps, like in other DAWs).
- Tools: `arm_track({track_id, input_channel, armed?})`, `set_punch({in_bar, out_bar, pre_roll_bars, enabled?})`, `record_audio` uses them (`use_setup`, default true) and lists every recorded clip. 129 tools.

### F24 · Drummer and CC0 loop library

### F23 · Time-stretch and pitch-shift of audio clips
- `src/audio/stretch.ts` (pure, no dependency, no GPL code — Rubber Band is not used): **WSOLA** for stretching (≈23 ms frames, waveform-similarity search; frames containing an attack are placed so that the attack lands exactly on its stretched time) and a **phase vocoder with identity phase locking** plus cubic resampling for pitch. Runs in a worker (`workers/stretch.worker.ts`) with a cache of processed buffers by (sample, factor, semitones) (`audio/processed.ts`), used by the live engine and the offline render alike; 3 ms fades at both ends.
- Model: `AudioClip.stretch?: {mode: off | follow_tempo | fixed, ratio?, originalBpm?}` and `pitch?` (semitones, decimals = cents). follow_tempo: the clip plays at the song tempo from its original BPM, keeping its length in beats when the tempo changes; fixed: played length / original length.
- Tempo of a loop, estimated at import (kept on the clip, stretch off): from the file name (`loop_92bpm.wav`, `Funk 120 BPM.wav`) or from an onset envelope's autocorrelation, snapped to a whole number of bars when close. `detect_tempo({sample_id | region_id, apply?})`.
- Clip editor: *Stretch* (Off / Follow tempo (92 BPM) / Fixed ratio), *Detect BPM*, *Pitch*; waveforms show the stretched clip. Changing the key in the display offers *Transpose N audio clips ±k* (one undo step); `set_key({key, transpose_audio})` does it for the AI (the short way, up to a tritone).
- DAWproject: a warp map now becomes a fixed stretch (several markers are simplified to one ratio and reported); stretched clips export with the matching warp; pitch shifts are listed as not exportable.
- Tools: `update_region` takes `stretch_mode`, `ratio`, `original_bpm`, `pitch`; new `detect_tempo`; `set_key` takes `transpose_audio`. 123 tools.
- Decision record and comparison on three materials (voice-like vowel, drums, pad; transients kept, pitch error, edge jumps, speed): [docs/adr/0002-time-stretch.md](docs/adr/0002-time-stretch.md); `scripts/stretch-compare.mjs` regenerates it and writes WAVs for a listening test, which has **not** been done by people yet.
- Tests: 0.8× / 1.25× with the right length and no clicks at the edges or inside; a 100 BPM loop at 120 BPM with every onset within 5 ms of the grid (the spec asks ±10 ms); ±3/−5 semitones within 1% of the target pitch with the length kept; stretch and pitch together; tempo from audio (92, 128, 75 BPM within 1) and from names; the commands (follow_tempo keeps 2 bars at any tempo, fixed ratio, pitch, errors, key transposition). In Chrome: a 92 BPM loop imported into a 120 BPM song, detect_tempo + follow_tempo → exactly 2 bars, exported onsets 1–4 ms from the grid, playback through the worker without errors.

### F22 · Automation of effect and plugin parameters
- Schema v3: `Track.automation` is a list of lanes `{target, points}` with open targets — `{kind:'mix', param}` (volume, pan, tone, reverb, echo), `{kind:'fx', unit:'amp'|'eq'|'comp', param}` (drive, tone, level · low, mid, high, midFreq · threshold, ratio, attack, release, makeup) and `{kind:'plugin', slotId (null = the plugin instrument), paramId}`. Values in the parameter's own range (plugins: from `getParameterInfo`). Migration v2 → v3 converts the old `{volume: […], …}` records (tested); every module that read them (engine, render, structure edits, canonical form, DAWproject, the lane UI) goes through `src/automation.ts`.
- Live and offline: mix lanes as before; effect lanes on the built-in units at audio time (`FxChain.setParam`); plugin lanes as `wam-automation` events (`setParameterValues` where a plugin takes no events). Offline, plugin automation is sent during the render at each 20 ms step (with a little main-thread time per step) and automated plugins get a 0.5 s lead-in that is trimmed: posting every event before the render — what the transport does — made plugins apply them late, and a plugin starts from its saved state and glides from there. Without these the export was up to 4 dB off live in the first second; with them, the end-to-end check below passes.
- Recording automation: each lane has Read / Touch / Latch. While the song plays, moving any fader or knob of the mix or of the built-in effects writes its lane at the playhead (one undo step per gesture); Touch returns to the previous curve when you let go, Latch holds the last value until playback stops. AI commands never write. (Moves made inside a plugin's own window are not recorded.)
- UI: the lane selector lists Mix / Amp / EQ / Compressor and every loaded plugin's parameters, with a dot on those that have a curve; values shown in dB, Hz, ms, ratio or L/R. A lane key that is unknown or stale falls back to the volume lane instead of breaking the arrangement (a crash found while testing).
- Tools: `set_automation` takes a `target` (the `param` signature still works), `clear_automation` too; new `list_automatable({track_id})` with every target, its range and whether it has a curve; `get_project` lists curves by key (`volume`, `fx:eq.midFreq`, `plugin:<slot>:<param>`). 121 tools.
- `?debug` puts the engine and the store on `window.openband` (diagnostics and end-to-end tests); `engine.captureMaster(seconds)` records what the master plays.
- Tests: migration, target keys, `set_automation` with targets and clamping, touch and latch through the real store, plugin lanes as scheduled events (and the fallback). E2E `automation` (also in CI): a GraphicEqualizer high-pass swept 40 Hz → 3 kHz on a square wave, captured live and rendered: every 100 ms window within 1 dB (0.64–0.77 dB worst, 0.12 dB mean), while the export without the sweep is 5 dB off — so the check is sensitive. A detuned synth differs by ~1.2 dB between live and export even with nothing automated (random oscillator phase), which is why the test uses a square wave.

### F21 · Safety net: journal, crash recovery and time machine
- Journal (IndexedDB store `journal`, DB v4, indexed by song): every step that closes — an edit, an AI request, undo, redo, opening a song — appends `{at, label, source, patch}`. The patch is a structural JSON diff (`src/journal/patch.ts`: objects key by key, arrays of objects with ids — tracks, regions, notes, sections — by id, so moving a note stores one small op); a full checkpoint every 50 entries or 10 minutes; everything gzipped. (The F13 diff in `versions/diff.ts` describes changes for people; the journal needs one that can be applied, hence its own.)
- Crash recovery: at start, when the journal holds a later state than the autosave, a banner says "I recovered 14 changes made after the last save" with Restore (one undo step) / Ignore.
- Time machine (Versions panel): a slider over everything journaled; *Preview* opens the song as it was in the compare view (colours, A/B listening against now), *Restore this moment* replaces the song (undoable), *Open as branch* saves it as a version on a new branch and checks it out.
- Automatic versions "Before: …" ahead of destructive operations: `new_project`, `import_project_json`, `checkout`, `merge`, `delete_track` with regions, `restore_at`, and AI requests reaching 21 commands (the state before the request). Nothing is saved for an empty song or one equal to the current version; when the operation replaces the song, the version goes to the song it was taken from. (`import_dawproject` will use it too, with F17.)
- Exports never overwrite: saving `song.openband` again in this browser gives `song v2.openband`, `song v3.openband`…
- Retention: 30 days or 200 MB per song; the state at the new start becomes a checkpoint, so everything after it can still be rebuilt.
- Tools: `get_timeline({from?, to?})`, `preview_at({time})` (a summary like get_project; time as ISO, ms or relative "-20m"), `restore_at({time, as_branch?})` (destructive). 118 tools.
- Tests: patch round-trips; 500 random commands through the real command API (tracks, regions, notes, moves, automation, sections, undo/redo…) with the state rebuilt at every instant equal to the recorded one, also after reloading from storage and after compaction; a simulated crash (journal 14 steps ahead of the autosave) recovered exactly, as one undo step; no banner when the autosave is current; export names. ### F20 · Monitoring essentials
- Live LUFS meter on the master and on every track: an AudioWorklet (`src/audio/worklets/loudness.worklet.ts`) running the streaming BS.1770 DSP (`loudness-dsp.js`: 100 ms blocks → momentary 400 ms, short-term 3 s, gated integrated since reset, 4× oversampled true peak with hold, reset). The DSP is plain JavaScript loaded as text into the worklet, so no bundler transform can break it there (the first version, a TS class copied with `toString()`, failed in the production build because of class-field helpers — caught in the browser check). Tested against the offline `loudness.ts` on sines, noise, a signal with loud and quiet parts (gating) and a two-tone K-weighting case: integrated and momentary within 0.3 LU, true peak within 0.1–0.15 dB.
- **Fix:** the K-weighting filters (offline and live) are now the standard bilinear-transform design (as libebur128), reproducing the coefficients published in BS.1770 at 48 kHz; before, an RBJ-style derivation read 0.26 dB low (a 0 dBFS 997 Hz sine gave −3.27 instead of −3.01 LUFS). All loudness numbers (analyze_mix, reference matching, the linter) move up by about 0.26 LU.
- Target selector (Streaming −14, Apple −16, Broadcast −23, Club −8, with their true-peak ceilings) and a green / yellow / red light (within 1 LU / 3 LU / beyond or over the ceiling), remembered in the browser. The panel sits next to the master strip.

### F19 · Undo that includes plugin state
- A plugin's whole state (`getState()`: presets, modes, wavetables, anything its GUI changes — not only automatable parameters) is now part of every undo step: it lives in the project (`fx.plugins[].state`, the instrument's `wam.state`), so each history snapshot carries it (`src/pluginHistory.ts`).
- GUI edits: while a plugin window is open its state is polled every 750 ms and hashed (CRC32 of a key-sorted JSON); a change opens an automatic "Edit <plugin>" step, extended while you keep tweaking and closed after 1.5 s of quiet. The first reading after opening is the baseline: opening or closing the window never makes a step.
- Undo, redo, checkout and loading apply the project's state to the live plugins with `setState` when it differs from what they have; meanwhile polling is paused and the studio shows a spinner over everything, so the restore can't be mistaken for an edit or raced by a click. What the plugin reports after `setState` becomes the new baseline (plugins may serialise differently from what they were given).
- `set_plugin_params` (AI) records the resulting state as a normal edit: undoable, grouped with the rest of the AI's request.
- Memory: history keeps at most 50 MB of plugin state, newest first; older steps lose it (`pluginStateDropped`, `plugin_state_dropped` in `get_history`) and undoing to them leaves plugins as they are. States are not compressed in history (the cap bounds the memory instead).
- `get_history` lists `plugins_changed` (plugin names) for every step. No new tools.
- Tests with a fake WAM whose state is not just parameters: GUI edit (several polls) → one step; undo → the exact original state; redo → the edited one; no step from opening/closing the window or from applying a state; `set_plugin_params` undoable; unrelated edits don't touch the plugin; the 50 MB cap (newest kept). Checked in Chrome with a catalog plugin (Simple Delay): opening its window adds no step, `set_plugin_params` 0.3 → 4.5 is undone back to exactly 0.3 and redone.
- The plugin parameter events (`wam-automation`) suggested by the spec are not used: polling covers every plugin, including those that don't send them.

### F18 · Flub markers
- While recording, **F** marks a mistake and **G** a good part (with musical typing open, where F and G play notes: Shift+F / Shift+G); outside a recording F still opens Effects. A MIDI pedal marks mistakes too: CC64 by default, or any controller learned from the *To fix* panel (*Learn*; kept in this browser), only on the way down and only while recording.
- Model: `Project.flubs?: Flub[]` (`{id, beat, trackId, regionId, takeId, kind: flub|good|note, text, createdAt, resolved, author}`), optional, no schema bump. Marks made during a take wait and are stored with the recorded region in the same undo step, bound to it and — in cycle recording — to the take of their pass; a take that records nothing keeps its marks. Marks follow their region when it moves or changes track; deleting the region keeps them unlinked.
- *To fix* panel: the list with bar.beat, track and take, jump (2-bar loop around the mark), *Best take*, *Fixed*, delete. `]` / `[` jump to the next / previous open mistake with that loop. Red, green and grey flags on the ruler, inside the region (for the take that plays) and on the take lanes of the Takes dialog.
- *Best take* (`replace_with_best_take`): among the takes with no mistake within ±1 beat of the mark, the one with the most good marks (then fewer mistakes, then the newest) is comped in over the bars around the mark — the rest stays the active take; audio takes switch as a whole. The mark becomes resolved; one undo step.
- Tools: `add_flub`, `list_flubs`, `resolve_flub`, `delete_flub` (destructive), `replace_with_best_take`; a `flub` studio event for each mark (get_events / wait_for_event), and the server instructions tell the AI to offer fixes. 112 tools.

- Reference projects in `src/__fixtures__/`: the v0.1 demo exported as real v1 JSON, an empty project and a deliberately "dirty" one for the linter.
- CI workflow (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`): typecheck and tests on every push and pull request.
- The WAM SDK is now loaded lazily so the command and store modules can be imported in Node.

## 0.1.0

First public version: arrangement, piano roll, step sequencer, synthesized instruments, imported sounds (sampler, drum kits, SoundFont, audio tracks), per-track effects, automation, MIDI import/export, WAM plugins, WAV export, English/Italian UI and AI control over MCP.
