# ADR 0003 — C2PA Content Credentials in exported audio

Status: accepted — not now; revisit on the triggers below (E6) · 2026-10-29

## Context

OpenBand records **who made what** in the contribution ledger (SPEC v0.6 §6): engine, model, licence, the
corrections it applied, and the human / AI share per track. That record already reaches people in three
ways:
- the Release Kit's credits and AI-use statement (G6);
- the attributions that CC BY weights require, added by themselves;
- the work's manifest, which a Bitcoin timestamp anchors (G7; from v0.6.41 it includes the ledger).

The spec asked us to evaluate embedding it as **C2PA Content Credentials** in the exported files. C2PA is
the open standard for media provenance, and the point was to check how well audio formats support it
before committing.

What the sources say (C2PA spec 2.1; the CAI's c2pa-rs and c2pa-js; IPTC; CAWG; checked on 2026-10-29):

- **Formats.**
  - The spec defines embedding in RIFF (a `C2PA` chunk at the end of a WAV), in ID3v2 (a `GEOB` frame, for
    MP3) and in ISO BMFF (a `uuid` box before the first `mdat`, for M4A/MP4).
  - c2pa-rs lists wav, mp3, flac, m4a and mp4 as supported.
  - **Ogg/Opus is not supported**, and neither the spec nor the SDK covers it (Ogg Vorbis is only "being
    considered").
  - How FLAC is embedded (ID3 or native) we could not confirm.
- **Signing.**
  - A manifest is signed with an X.509 end-entity certificate: ECDSA, RSA-PSS or Ed25519, with
    `digitalSignature` and an email-protection or document-signing EKU.
  - "The CAI SDK does not allow you to use a self-signed certificate to sign a manifest."
  - Validators show any signer that is not on their trust lists as untrusted
    (`signingCredential.untrusted`, "the issuer couldn't be recognized").
  - Conforming generators "must use a certificate that can be traced back to a certificate on the C2PA
    trust list". That means a commercial CA from that list (four were named in April 2026) and the
    C2PA conformance process, with a legal agreement and evidence.
  - Signing every user's files would need either a key in the app, which is not safe, or a signing
    service run by the project.
  - Timestamps come from an RFC 3161 TSA on the TSA trust list.
- **Libraries.**
  - c2pa-rs is MIT / Apache-2.0, beta (0.x, breaking changes about every two months).
  - c2pa-node is a native addon that needs a patched Node.
  - c2pa-web runs in the browser, but its documentation shows no signing.
  - OpenBand's studio is a web page first, and its exports are made in the browser.
- **Vocabulary.**
  - `c2pa.created` with an IPTC `digitalSourceType` fits generated music well:
    `trainedAlgorithmicMedia`, `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia`, `compositeSynthetic`,
    `algorithmicMedia` (our builtins) and `digitalCreation`.
  - Samples can be ingredients with `componentOf`.
  - The CAWG training-and-data-mining assertion (`cawg.training-mining`) can say whether the work may be
    used to train models.
- **Reach.** We found **no source** that any music platform or player reads C2PA in audio, keeps it on
  upload, or shows it. Platforms re-encode uploads, and a re-encode breaks the hard binding, the hash of
  the asset's bytes. The spec's own fallback for lost metadata is a sidecar `.c2pa` file or a
  `Link: rel=c2pa-manifest` header.

## Decision

**We don't embed C2PA in exported audio now.** Without a certificate on the C2PA trust list, every
manifest OpenBand wrote would show as untrusted, or not validate at all, in the tools that read it. That
would be worse than nothing for a provenance claim. Getting such a certificate means the project, not
the user, enters a conformance program and runs a signing service. And for audio, the files it would
protect lose the manifest at the first upload, while no platform we know reads it.

What carries provenance instead, all from the same ledger:
- **The credits and AI-use statement in the Release Kit** (G6): human-readable, travel with the release.
- **The work's manifest in the G7 proof** (`work.json`, timestamped in Bitcoin). From v0.6.41 it includes
  the ledger: the human / AI share per track, the engines and their licences, the attributions and the
  ledger's hash. Anyone can verify it without OpenBand (`ots verify`, `sha256sum`), and it doesn't depend
  on metadata surviving inside the audio.
- **The ledger itself** in `.openband` and DAWproject files.

When we do it, the plan is ready:
- a `c2pa.created` action with the IPTC source type computed from the ledger: `digitalCreation` with no
  engine parts; `compositeWithTrainedAlgorithmicMedia` or `compositeSynthetic` for a mix; and
  `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` when engines made all of it;
- samples and context audio as ingredients;
- the CAWG training-mining assertion from the licence chooser;
- WAV, FLAC and MP3 from the Release Kit (not Ogg);
- signed by the local OpenBand (Node), never in the page.

## Revisit when

- a music platform reads or preserves C2PA in uploaded audio (or a distributor asks for it), or
- the C2PA trust list admits a path a free, open-source project can follow (for example a free CA for
  open-source generators, or per-user identity through CAWG that doesn't need a trust-list certificate
  for the claim generator), or
- c2pa-web can sign in the browser with a user-held key that validators accept.

## Consequences

- No new dependency, nothing to sign, no key to protect. Exports are byte-for-byte what they were.
- Provenance is verifiable today through G7, outside the audio files, and survives re-encoding (the
  proof is about the master as stamped).
- If a platform starts requiring Content Credentials, OpenBand cannot provide them until the work above is
  done.
